# The Widowed Throne

A Christian fantasy RPG setting · Sep 27, 2026 · @Jason

## Premise

The Widowed Throne is a fantasy of Byzantium a hundred years after God's anointed line lost the crown. The purple throne in the City stands empty. Rebel powers of the heavens hold the provinces, and the Nephilim have come back to rule the mountain passes and the plains.

The countryside lives like the days of the Judges. No emperor reigns, each lord does what seems right in his own eyes, and God raises deliverers from small houses in hard years. The City lives like the days of Ezra and Nehemiah. A remnant came home from exile thirty years ago, and its people rebuild the walls with a trowel in one hand and a spear in the other.

Players take the part of the faithful who stand in the breach. They fight monsters with lance and sword, yet the deeper war concerns allegiance. A demon draws strength from human worship, and a giant draws his from human fear. Heroes win when they break both.

### Tone pillars

- Hope under ruin: the biblical story bends toward restoration. Let the table feel that pull in the darkest session.
- Evil twists good: demons create nothing. They bend beauty, wealth, desire and power into chains.
- Heaven and earth overlap: holy mountains and consecrated altars mark thin places where the spiritual realm shows through.
- God sets the terms: heroes pray and obey, and angels move at God's word. The party does not summon or command them.

## Cosmology

The setting runs on the biblical picture of two realms that overlap. Earth lies below as the domain of humans. The heavens lie above as the domain of God and the spiritual beings who serve him. The two meet at high and holy places, the way Eden stood as a mountain garden where heaven and earth were one.

### Thin places

The people of this world call them topoi hagioi, the holy places. A mountain monastery counts, and so does the sanctuary behind a church's icon screen. Angels and cherubim cross into the world at thin places, and rebels try to seize them. A thin place matters as much as a fortress, and the campaign treats it that way.

### The Divine Council

God shares his rule. He set humans over the earth, and he set the members of his heavenly council over the nations. Scripture pictures this council in the stars: the host of heaven, the sons of God, the rulers and authorities.

At Babel, God scattered the nations and handed them to these council members. Many rebelled. They wanted worship for themselves, and they taught the nations to serve money, desire, war and their own pride.

In this setting, each theme (province) of the empire has a heavenly ruler called an archon. A loyal archon guards its people and points them to God. A rebel archon feeds on their worship and bends their rulers into its image.

### The victory of Christ

The story takes place after the cross. Christ disarmed the powers and triumphed over them, and all authority in heaven and on earth belongs to him. The rebels lost their claim, yet they linger and hold ground where people hand them allegiance. They lose that ground where people take it back.

### The anointed emperor

Byzantine faith held the emperor to be an earthly image of the heavenly King. In this world the belief carries weight in both realms. An emperor anointed by God's prophet with the Horn of Anointing stands as Christ's steward over the empire. While such an emperor reigns in faithfulness, the rebel archons cannot keep their thrones. The empty throne gave the themes to the rebels, and a new anointing would shake their hold on all of them.

## History

The empire rose under a shepherd whom a prophet anointed and fell under his proud heirs. It waits for a son of his line to take the throne again.

| Years ago | Era | Events |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present | The Days of Judges | The Land Walls stand. The Great Church has no roof. The themes answer to archons and giants, or to whatever deliverer God raises up. |
| 30 | The Return | After seventy years in exile, a remnant came home under the priest Esdras and the governor Neemias. They found the City burned and rebuilt the Land Walls in fifty-two days while rival lords mocked them from the hills. |
| 100 | The Fall of the City | Emperor Andronikos the Proud set his own statue inside the Great Church. The Horn of Anointing vanished that night. Within a year Nebrodes, a Nephilim king of the eastern plains, broke the walls, burned the Great Church, and marched the faithful to his tower-city of Nebrodia. |
| 260 to 100 | The Shepherd's House | Seven heirs of the Shepherd ruled in turn, and each gave ground to the archons. The palace minted coin in honour of the Sea-Dragon, and the armies marched under the banner of the Prince of the Heights. |
| 300 to 260 | The Shepherd's Reign | Abba Symeon the Stylite came down from his pillar and anointed Poimen, youngest son of a hill-country herdsman, with oil from the Horn. Poimen killed the giant Gathos at the Cilician Gates with a sling stone, united the themes, and reigned forty years. |

### The Shepherd's Promise

Before he died, Symeon gave Poimen a word from God: a son of the Shepherd will sit on the throne again, and the Horn will find him. Poimen's blood survives in forgotten houses across the themes. Most heirs do not know it, and several lords would kill to learn their names.

The Horn of Anointing did not break or burn. It hid itself on the night of the statue, and it waits for the one God chooses.

## The fallen themes

Five themes and one enemy realm make up the map. Each rebel archon rules through an idol, and the idol shapes the theme's look, its villains and its temptations.

| Region | Real-world model | Archon | Idol | Power on the ground |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The City (Theopolis) | Constantinople | None holds it; the Adversary works in its councils | Self-rule | Eparch Irene Doukaina and Patriarch Esdras the Younger |
| Thema of the Waters | Aegean islands | Rahab the Sea-Dragon | Money | Leon Chrysocheir (Goldhand), merchant-prince of the island ports |
| Thema of the Gardens | Thessalonike and Hellas | The Queen of Heaven | Desire | The Festival Council of Thessalonike |
| Thema of the Heights | Cappadocia | The Prince of the Heights | War | Anak son of Gathos, a giant warlord |
| Thema of the Wilderness | Syria and Egypt | Azazel of the Wastes | Despair and death | No lord; desert monasteries and raider clans |
| Nebrodia | Mesopotamia, beyond the border | The Lord of the Tower | Pride | Nebrodes the Hunter, giant high king |

### The City

The remnant holds the Land Walls and a third of the old streets. The Eparch runs the watch and the grain, and the Patriarch keeps the liturgy alive under the open sky of the Great Church. No archon claims the City, so the Adversary works there by slander: accusations in council and forged letters between factions.

### Thema of the Waters

Fog banks swallow ships that refuse to pay the Goldhand's tithe. The merchant-prince burns incense to the Sea-Dragon in a cave-temple under his harbour, and his coin carries a coiled serpent. Island folk grow rich and afraid in equal measure.

### Thema of the Gardens

Thessalonike looks like paradise: orchards and fountains, with a feast on the calendar each week. The Queen of Heaven promises plenty to those who bake her cakes and burn her incense. The poor vanish from the festival crowds, and the orphanages empty year by year.

### Thema of the Heights

Christians in Cappadocia live in cave churches and underground cities carved into the soft rock. Above them, Anak son of Gathos holds the passes with a war-host of giants and bandits. He demands a champion each spring, and no champion has returned alive.

### Thema of the Wilderness

The desert belongs to Azazel, the dark creature of the waste places, and to the goat-demons that dance in ruined cities. The desert fathers and mothers still keep their cells here, fighting the demons of thought in the heat of noon. Pilgrims cross the Wilderness to reach its holy mountain, the one thin place the rebels have not taken.

### Nebrodia

Nebrodes the Hunter claims descent from the sons of God and wears the imperial purple he took from the City. His tower-city rises on the plain where the first Babel stood, and giant kings from far lands send him tribute. He means to finish the tower, and his seers say an anointing in the City would stop him.

## The rebels

Spiritual evil in this setting distorts what God made good and drags creation back toward darkness and disorder. It works on two levels: behind corrupt powers and inside single human lives.

### The Adversary

The Greeks of the City call him ho Satanas, the Adversary, or ho Diabolos, the Slanderer. Both are titles. He has lost his name because he stands for nothing and against everything. Scripture shows him as a serpent, a sea dragon, a desert creature and the king of death in the grave.

The Adversary has no stat block. He appears as a voice in a dream, a serpent on a tomb, or a smooth adviser at a lord's table. He works through lies and accusation, turning allies against each other. The heroes cannot slay him in this age; they can expose his lies and refuse his offer.

### Demons

Moses first called the rebel host demons, lesser spiritual beings. The setting sorts them into four kinds.

| Kind | Scale | What they do | How heroes defeat them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Archons | A theme | Fallen council members bound to a region and its idol. They rule through human lords. | The people of the theme turn their allegiance back to God. Swords do not touch them. |
| Daimones | A person or household | Lesser spirits that exploit greed and selfishness, and the weakness of mortal bodies. Desert monks name them after the passions: the demon of avarice, the demon of anger, the noonday demon of despair. | Exorcism in the name of Christ, followed by filling the empty house with good. |
| Serpents and dragons | A lair or a sea | Burning seraph-serpents who once served at the throne, sea dragons of Rahab's brood, and goat-demons of the waste places. | Holy relics, blessed weapons and faithful champions. They can be driven off or chained in the abyss. |
| Rephaim | A barrow or battlefield | Shades of dead giants who climb out of Sheol where death has feasted. | Burial rites, prayer and fire. |

The Gospel warns that an unclean spirit cast out of a house returns with seven worse if it finds the house swept and empty. Use that rule at the table. A demon driven from a village comes back stronger unless the heroes leave faith, justice and care in its place.

### The Nephilim

In the days before the flood, rebel sons of God took human wives, and their children grew into violent warrior giants. The ancient kingdoms claimed such giants as founders: half human, half divine, full of stolen wisdom. The Bible names them rebels captive to spiritual evil.

On the night Andronikos set up his statue, the Lord of the Tower opened a door the flood had shut. Rebels took brides again in the courts of Nebrodia, and giants walked out of the tower within a generation.

- Anakim: warlord giants, three to four metres tall, who carve out kingdoms and demand worship as god-kings.
- Gibborim: the mighty men of renown, smaller giants who serve as champions, bodyguards and executioners.
- Nephilim seers: giants who inherited their fathers' stolen wisdom. They read omens and work sorcery for Nebrodes.
- Giant-blooded: humans with a trace of the old blood, prized by lords as shock troops.

Giants are flesh. They bleed and die. They grow stronger on fear and boasting, and they fall to small things: a shepherd's sling, a tent peg, a millstone dropped by a woman from a tower.

## The heavenly host

The loyal host serves God's aim: to bring humans back into his presence in a reunited heaven and earth. Angels play a supporting role in that story, and the setting keeps them there.

### Angels

Angel means messenger. Angels in Scripture have no wings. They look like people, a little taller and brighter, and travellers mistake them for strangers on the road. They carry messages from God and carry out missions for him, like the angel who walked Peter out of prison.

The named angels carry names that point to God. Michael means "who is like God?" The Byzantines call him the Archistrategos, commander of the heavenly armies, and his icon guards the gates of the City. Gabriel means "God is my power."

Rules for angels at the table:

- Angels act on God's initiative. Prayer can ask for help; it cannot summon a named angel.
- Scripture does not encourage anyone to go looking for angels, and the heroes should not. A seeker of angels makes easy prey.
- The Adversary can disguise himself as an angel of light. A radiant visitor who flatters a lord or urges him to seize the throne is a demon in costume.
- People who meet an angel feel fear and confusion first. Play the encounter that way.

### Cherubim

Cherubim guard the boundary between heaven and earth. Each one looks like a collage of animals, and no two sightings match. They stood at the gate of Eden with a flaming sword, and their images covered the walls of the tabernacle and temple. The Ark of the Covenant bore two cherubim as the footstool of God's throne.

In this setting, cherubim guard the thin places. They do not fight for the heroes. They bar the way to anyone who comes in rebellion, and they let the humble through. The sanctuary of the roofless Great Church has a cherub at its threshold that no one has seen, and the Patriarch hears its wings at night.

### Seraphim

The seraphim, the burning ones, surround the throne and cry holy. They appear in visions, in the manner of Isaiah's call. A hero who sees them receives a commission and a coal of purification.

### The Angel of the Lord

One figure stands apart. The Angel of the Lord speaks as God and accepts worship. The church fathers read him as the Son before his birth in Bethlehem. He wrestles with Jacob and stands in Balaam's road with a drawn sword.

Keep him for the turning points of a campaign: once or twice in a campaign, and not as an ally on call. He might appear as a stranger at a river ford who wrestles a hero until dawn and leaves him limping and renamed.

### Deliverance-style battles

For games that want the board-game feel of Deliverance, run spiritual battles on two layers. The heroes fight on the earthly layer with steel. Angels and demons fight on the heavenly layer, and the heroes affect it through prayer and acts of faithfulness. A victory on one layer tips the odds on the other. The GM controls the angels; the players control what they give their allegiance to.

## The Tagma of the Horn

The Tagma of the Horn is a small order of armoured holy warriors sworn to find the Horn of Anointing and the heir it will choose. Its members call themselves Hornbearers. They fight like Byzantine cataphracts and live under vows like monks, in the tradition of the warrior saints George, Demetrios, Theodore and Mercurios.

### The founder: Pelagios the Fool

Pelagios grew up in the Pontic forests with a widowed mother who hid him from war after it took his father and brothers. He knew nothing of lords or courts. At sixteen he saw two cataphracts ride past, took them for angels, and followed them to the City in armour he pulled off a dead raider.

The nobles laughed at him. The Patriarch saw a holy fool, a salos, and blessed him.

On his first quest Pelagios sheltered in the ruined hall of a crippled lord in the hills of Thrace. The lord could not walk; a wound in his thigh had festered for twenty years, and his lands lay barren around him. At the evening meal a procession passed through the hall: a youth carrying a horn of oil that shone like a lamp, followed by a girl carrying a broken sceptre. Pelagios had learned that a good guest keeps quiet, so he asked nothing. At dawn the hall stood empty and in ruins.

He searched for twenty years. The second time he found the hall, he asked the question he had swallowed: "Whom will the Horn anoint?" The crippled lord wept, and his wound closed. He told Pelagios that he came from the Shepherd's line, lame like Mephibosheth, and that the Horn waits for the one God chooses. By morning the lord had died in peace, and his fields had turned green.

Pelagios founded the Tagma at the Land Walls that year. He lives still, old and blind, and the Hornbearers call him Abba.

### The Question

The oath of a Hornbearer ends with the founder's question: "Whom will the Horn anoint?" Hornbearers ask it aloud at each shrine and thin place they enter. Most of the time silence answers. Now and then a thin place answers with a vision or a stranger at the door.

### Arms and armour

Hornbearers fight as heavy cavalry. They wear lamellar over mail, ride horses in felt barding, and carry the kontarion lance, the spathion sword and a steel mace for giants. A leather sling hangs from each belt in memory of the Shepherd.

The emblem shows a horn of oil above a sling stone, painted white on a purple field. Purple belongs to emperors, and the order wears it as a promise it holds in trust for the heir.

### The armour of God

The order blesses each piece of a knight's harness with a virtue from Paul's letter to the Ephesians and inscribes the virtue on the metal.

| Piece | Virtue | Guards against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Belt | Truth | Lies and slander of the Adversary |
| Breastplate | Justice | Corruption and bribes |
| Boots | The gospel of peace | Fear and despair |
| Shield | Faithfulness | Burning arrows of temptation |
| Helmet | Salvation | Accusation and shame |
| Sword | The word of God | The knight's one weapon against spirits |

A piece protects the knight while the knight keeps its virtue. A Hornbearer who lies finds the belt heavy and cold.

### Chapter houses

- The Fool's Tower on the Land Walls of the City, the mother house.
- Saint George of the Rock, a cave monastery in Cappadocia under the nose of Anak son of Gathos.
- A hidden house in the Wilderness beside the holy mountain.
- The Harbour Chapel on Naxos, a two-knight outpost that watches the Goldhand's fleet.

### Factions

The order carries a quarrel at its heart. The Shepherd faction holds that God chooses the heir and the order must search and wait. The Sword faction argues that the empire will die while the order waits, and that the Hornbearers should crown the strongest faithful lord and let God bless the choice. The Domestikos leans toward the Sword. The Abba says nothing and asks his question.

## Heroes and rules hooks

The setting runs on any fantasy system. The archetypes map onto familiar classes, and the rules hooks add the setting's spiritual war on top.

### Archetypes

| Archetype | Byzantine model | Class it replaces | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hornbearer | Cataphract of the Tagma | Paladin | Heavy cavalry, protector, exorcist-in-armour |
| Akrites | Digenis Akritas, the border hero | Ranger | Scout and giant-hunter of the frontier themes |
| Abba or Amma | Desert fathers and mothers | Cleric or monk | Discernment, exorcism, fasting and prayer |
| Iconographer | Icon painters of the monasteries | Artificer | Paints icons that open or seal thin places |
| Holy fool | The salos of Byzantine saints' lives | Bard or trickster | Speaks hard truth to lords; immune to flattery and charm |
| Anargyros | The unmercenary physician-saints | Healer | Heals for free; loses power if paid |
| Deliverer | The judges of Israel | Any | An ordinary person God raises up for one task, with sudden strength when it arrives |

### Allegiance

Track each theme's allegiance on a scale from Bowed to Returned. The archon's strength scales with it. Heroes move the track through public acts: freeing captives, breaking an idol in the market square, restoring a church, judging a dispute with justice. A theme that reaches Returned casts out its archon, and the heavenly layer changes hands.

### Footholds

Paul warns against giving the devil a foothold. Each hero tracks four idols: Gold, Flesh, Sword and Crown. A hero who takes an idol's easy path gains a boost now and hands its demon a Foothold. Demons spend Footholds to tempt or accuse that hero at the worst moment. Confession and restitution remove them.

### Prayer

Heroes pray; they do not cast spells. The player states the prayer, and the GM answers with yes, no, wait, or a different gift. Grant a Grace die for faithful prayer that fits the story. Miracles come from God's side of the table, and the GM should make them surprising.

### Casting out

Demons do not die. A successful exorcism drives a demon out of a person or place. Follow the Gospel rule: if the heroes leave the house empty, the demon returns with seven worse spirits within a season.

### Giants fall to small things

Humble weapons deal full damage to Nephilim: slings, tent pegs, millstones, a farmer's goad. Boasting before a giant feeds it. A hero who fights a giant in the Lord's name and in weakness gains an edge; a hero who fights for glory loses it.

## Campaign spine and hooks

The default campaign follows the search for the Horn and the heir, the way Samuel searched among the sons of Jesse. It runs in four acts.

1. The Wall: the heroes defend the City while the remnant rebuilds. Mockers and false prophets attack from outside, and the Adversary's slander splits the council from inside. The act ends when the heroes learn of the crippled lord's line.
2. The Judges: the heroes free one theme at a time. Each theme holds a clue to the Horn's hiding place and a hidden heir of the Shepherd's blood.
3. The Tower: Nebrodes marches on the City with his giant host to stop the anointing. The Sword faction of the Tagma crowns its own candidate to meet him.
4. The Anointing: the Horn reveals itself and passes over the strong candidates. It chooses the least likely heir, the youngest and the one out in the fields, who might be a hero at the table or a servant the party has ignored for three acts.

The emperor points beyond himself. Keep the ending honest to the story the setting borrows: the anointed emperor serves as a sign of Christ's reign, and the final hope rests on Christ making all things new.
